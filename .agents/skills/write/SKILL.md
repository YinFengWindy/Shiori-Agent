# Write

Write for the reader's task. Accept the intended audience, purpose, source facts, existing draft, and requested language. Preserve meaning and real uncertainty. This skill is complete on its own and combines document structure with editing for clarity.

## Choose the document's job

- Tutorial: guide a learner through visible results.
- How-to: give the steps and conditions for a concrete task.
- Reference: organize facts where readers expect to find them.
- Explanation: explain a bounded question, decisions, evidence, and tradeoffs.

Lead with what the reader can do or understand. For a README, show a useful first prompt or command and a concrete example before detailed internal rules. Link deeper instructions. Follow repository templates where applicable.

## Make the writing specific

Use real project terms, symbols, and commands. Replace vague claims with a mechanism, observation, example, source, or decision. Cut claims that carry no information.

Prefer familiar words and active verbs. Avoid generic praise, canned openings, filler conclusions, invented metaphors, unnecessary contrasts, synonym cycling, and decorative emphasis. Keep complete readable sentences; concision must not erase the conditions that make a claim true.

Use lists for parallel items and numbered steps for sequences. Put conditions before dependent instructions. Match the user's language rather than imposing English punctuation or word-count rules on another language.

## Preserve evidence and scope

For specs and designs, separate confirmed behavior, decisions and reasons, assumptions, exclusions, and acceptance criteria. Cite the authoritative source rather than copying whole documents.

For PRs and delivery reports, explain the concrete problem, resulting behavior, necessary implementation detail, relevant verification, and material limits. Link detailed logs. Do not turn tentative evidence into certainty or describe a planned action as completed.

Write and edit as one pass where possible, then check factual claims, links, and examples against their sources. Return or update the requested artifact. Writing does not authorize publication, add approvals, or expand the underlying work.
